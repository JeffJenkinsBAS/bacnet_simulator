# Command Center and Building Digital Twin

## Purpose

The command center is the instructor-facing view of the ACI BACnet
simulator. It turns the underlying point registry into an interactive
building digital twin that can be explored during WebCTRL training,
troubleshooting demonstrations, and showcase sessions.

The building arrangement is intentionally representative rather than
site-specific. It is a **typical training layout** selected to make plant,
air-handler, exhaust, zone, and pressure relationships easy to see. It is
not an as-built drawing, construction design, equipment schedule,
ventilation or energy calculation, controls submittal, or approved
sequence of operations.

## VAV zone strategy

The command center presents VAV-1 through VAV-17 as one fleet, but the
zone-temperature source is deliberately split:

- **VAV-1 and VAV-2 use external zone-temperature controllers.** These
  retain the connection to the physical test-bench wall controllers and
  let students observe real controller-to-simulator BACnet interaction
  even though no physical HVAC equipment is attached.
- **VAV-3 through VAV-17 are virtual zones.** Their temperatures and
  airside response are generated by the simulator, which gives an
  instructor enough scale to demonstrate diversity, simultaneous loads,
  aggregate airflow, and fault isolation without additional hardware.
  VAV-3 through VAV-15 additionally publish Zone Humidity at local `AI:3`;
  VAV-16 and VAV-17 remain temperature-only as requested.

This split preserves the showcase value of the wall controllers while
making the larger floorplate repeatable and resettable.

## Animated air delivery

Each VAV space has a source-backed image plume that loops only while the
terminal has proven, meaningful airflow. The API derives the visual state
from actual discharge-air temperature, zone temperature, flow, valve
position, and parent-equipment availability:

| Air color | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Blue | Useful cooling from mechanical cooling or economizer operation |
| Red | Useful hot-water reheat with the valve open and plant available |
| White/gray | Proven ventilation airflow without material heating or cooling |
| None | No proven/meaningful delivery |

Air is active only with AHU proof and at least the greater of 50 CFM or 10%
of design airflow. Cooling/heating requires a 2-degree F temperature
difference from the zone, with hysteresis to prevent flicker. Selecting a
VAV shows the conditioning source, CFM, DAT, zone temperature, sensible
effect, and dependency state in the inspector.

Space plume width, height, angle, and offset are configured independently in
`config/building_layout.json`; their screen position follows the equipment
location. The generated source asset is
`static/assets/airflow-wisp.png`.

## Airside pressure and exhaust behavior

The expanded airside model exposes two points used together for
positive-pressure training:

- **Building Pressure AI** reports the simulated building pressure.
- **Exhaust Fan VFD AO** accepts the exhaust-fan speed command.

The AHU supply fan contributes positive pressure. Enabled exhaust airflow
reduces it in proportion to the VFD command, while the simulated building
leakage/infiltration term returns pressure toward equilibrium. This lets a
WebCTRL or EIKON program demonstrate the intended control relationship:
modulate exhaust relative to supply to maintain a small positive building
pressure.

The simulator supplies the process response; it does not prescribe a
universal pressure setpoint or final control sequence. Setpoints,
deadbands, alarms, safeties, and tuning used in a real building must come
from that project's approved design.

### AHU-1 command center and duct-static PID lab

The command center includes a separate **AHU Command Center** view. WebCTRL
writes the active static-pressure setpoint to `AV:9002`; the simulator
publishes actual duct pressure at `AV:9003` and supply-fan VFD feedback at
`AV:9004`. `AV:9003` retains its identifier and now publishes a 0.00-10.00
in. H2O range.

The detailed graphic shows outside air entering through the economizer and
prefilter; return-air sensors/smoke and return fan entering from above; the
mixing plenum and mixed-air sensors; preheat coil and downstream serpentine
freezestat; cooling coil; reheat coil; supply fan; downstream supply-air
sensors/smoke; the high-static safety before a graphical duct break; and the
two-thirds duct-static station after the break. Animations follow actual
damper, valve, and fan state.

The pressure model uses the design-CFM-weighted feedback of all 17 VAV
dampers. At fixed fan speed, opening dampers lowers pressure and closing
dampers raises it. The direct-acting PID increases fan speed when opening
demand pulls pressure below setpoint.

The page exposes P, I, D, and calculation interval controls, live command and
proof, actual/setpoint/error, VFD output, aggregate VAV demand, an animated
air path, MA/RA/SA temperature and humidity, safety status, and a
setpoint-versus-actual trend. When the supply fan is not both commanded and
proven, the pressure process and trend are exactly zero.

The airside economizer card keeps the raw `AO:9023` WebCTRL request visible
beside the physically effective stroke. It reports free-cooling suitability,
the selected dual-enthalpy/single-enthalpy/dry-bulb method, OA and RA
enthalpy, enthalpy delta, OA dew point, mixed-air low-limit state,
full-open proof, integrated-cooling permission, limiting reason, and FDD
flags. These are command-center diagnostics, not additional BACnet objects.

A healthy high-static safety latches and stops the fans at 4.0 in. H2O. An
instructor-only safety-bypass/failure fault is required before pressure can
cross the representative 5.0-in. H2O training duct-class limit and trigger
the exploded-duct/red-flash state. A healthy freezestat prevents a
catastrophic low-temperature exposure; when explicitly bypassed, cooling-coil
entering air below 32 degrees F accumulates 20 simulated minutes without
useful chilled-water flow or 60 simulated minutes with cooling-valve command
and proven chilled-water flow. Freeze/burst/flood states latch until Restart.
See `docs/DUCT_STATIC_PID_LAB.md` for the complete point contract, state
machines, limitations, and cutover boundary.

## Fifteen-second diagnostics

The command center evaluates diagnostics whenever it refreshes. A mismatch
must persist for **15 wall-clock seconds** before the location is outlined
as a failure. For each VAV with a meaningful expected airflow, the
diagnostic compares actual airflow with the current WebCTRL setpoint and
available airside conditions.

- Within **±25%** of expected airflow: the VAV is considered to be
  tracking for the training display.
- Outside **±25%**: the VAV is highlighted for investigation after the
  condition persists for 15 wall-clock seconds.
- If the AHU is unable to provide air, the VAV is marked **inhibited** rather
  than failed so an upstream outage does not create seventeen false terminal
  failures.
- If AHU-1 cooling and heating valve commands remain materially open
  together, the AHU is outlined as an energy-waste failure after 15
  wall-clock seconds. A real cross-ramp is allowed for one actuator-travel
  window, including the time needed for the outgoing physical valve to
  finish closing after its command reaches zero.

The band is a teaching aid, not a construction tolerance or alarm limit.
An out-of-band result is a prompt to inspect damper command, actual
airflow, AHU fan state, static-pressure availability, forced values,
active faults, and zone demand. It is not proof of a failed terminal unit
by itself.

## Interactive operator workflow

The UI is designed for instructor-led exploration rather than passive
monitoring:

1. Start at the digital-twin overview to establish engine, BACnet,
   weather, pressure, and scenario state.
2. Select or search an equipment group to move from the building context
   to its live BACnet points.
3. Use the VAV fleet and diagnostic state to compare a suspect zone with
   normally tracking peers.
4. Open AHU Command Center to demonstrate fan-law response, tune the pressure
   loop, and compare healthy-safety behavior with a deliberate bypass lesson.
5. Move to Operations to activate an approved training scenario, inject
   a manual fault, force or release a value, or use STOP ALL.
6. Use the AI Console only for a reviewed proposal-and-approval workflow.
7. Confirm the student's conclusions in WebCTRL before revealing the
   simulator-side fault or scenario state.

The digital-twin artwork is illustrative. Live values, object identifiers,
fault state, and API responses remain the operational source of truth.

## 1024×768 support

The test-bench display can operate at 1024×768, while remote support may
use 1920×1080. At the smaller target:

- primary navigation and engine/scenario safety controls must remain
  reachable without browser zoom;
- dense cards and point tables may stack or scroll, but must not hide the
  current selection or action state;
- dialogs must fit within the viewport and keep their confirmation and
  cancel actions accessible;
- text, badges, focus indication, and touch/click targets must remain
  legible;
- decorative digital-twin detail may simplify before operational
  information is removed.

Any command-center change should be checked at both 1024×768 and
1920×1080 before deployment to the bench service.

The parent-equipment matrix, expected temperature ranges, air-color contract,
and remaining realism backlog are documented in
[`HVAC_REALISM_MODEL.md`](HVAC_REALISM_MODEL.md).

## Scope boundary

The digital twin supports technician training and demonstrations against
the isolated test bench. It does not authorize applying its layout,
diagnostic threshold, pressure target, point list, or sequences to a real
building. Real projects require verified drawings, equipment data,
engineering calculations, approved sequences, code review, and
site-specific commissioning.
